Strategy — Ride the Wave

This page ranks funds for a momentum-rotation approach: hold the funds that are currently growing strongly, and rotate as leadership changes.

It is a ranking of current strength, not a forecast. It cannot catch tops or bottoms; the aim is to capture the middle of sustained trends.

Momentum is the integral score over windows 5d, 10d, 21d, 64d, 129d (equal weight): nested windows weight recent performance more heavily. Higher means stronger, more sustained growth.

Risk-adjusted score = momentum ÷ volatilityα, where α is the “volatility influence” control below.

Stale funds (no new data for more than 10 days) are excluded.

Reading Momentum vs Volatility

Momentum = how strongly and consistently the fund has been rising (the size of the “wave”). Volatility = how bumpy that ride is — bigger daily swings mean larger drawdowns and more whipsaw risk.

Reason about the two together:

  • High momentum, low volatility — the ideal ride: a strong, steady climber. These rise to the top as you increase α.
  • High momentum, high volatility — strong but jumpy. Bigger potential upside, but prone to sharp reversals; treat as higher risk.
  • Low momentum, low volatility — calm but barely growing (cash-like). Not a wave worth riding; the trend gate filters most of these out.
  • Low momentum, high volatility — the worst combination: risky without reward. Avoid.

The Score column blends the two via α (score = momentum ÷ volatilityα). Raise α to favour steadier funds; lower it to chase raw strength.

The 1m / 3m / 6m returns show the trajectory: a fund strong over 6m but fading over 1m may be cresting — a cue to watch for rotation.

The Accel, Trend R² and Peak gap columns describe the shape of each fund's curve and do not affect the ranking:

  • Accel — recent daily pace minus the prior pace (percentage points per day over about a month). Positive means the trend is speeding up (catching a wave early); negative means it is decelerating or rolling over. A fund can have strong momentum yet negative acceleration: still high, but losing steam.
  • Trend R² — how clean the climb is, the R² of a straight-line fit over about a quarter (0 to 1). Near 1 is a smooth, reliable trend; a low value is the same net move via violent lurches. Read it with momentum's direction (a clean climb vs a clean fall).
  • Peak gap — how far below its own six-month high the fund sits. 0% means it is at a new high (breaking out); a negative value is how far it has faded off the peak.

Together these separate a fresh, accelerating breakout from a tiring leader drifting off its highs.

Remember this ranks current strength; it is not a forecast.

Worst performers — funds to avoid or exit

Worst performers

The flip side of riding the wave: the steepest decliners, the funds to avoid or exit. These are ranked by raw momentum (most negative first), not by the risk-adjusted score, because the volatility tilt is built for picking winners and would distort the bottom end.

Only funds in a downtrend (negative momentum) are listed. The shape columns still help: a fund that is declining and accelerating downward and far below its peak is a clear exit; one that is declining but decelerating or near a bottom may be turning. Funds you hold are flagged, so a held fund appearing here is a candidate to drop.
